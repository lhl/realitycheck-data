## Stage 3: Dialectical Analysis

### Steelmanned Argument
Hegemons often self-bind through institutions and norms to reduce resistance, increase legitimacy, and lower enforcement costs. If domestic politics shifts toward open coercion and rejects self-binding, the hegemon can become more dangerous even if its relative power is unchanged—because it uses power with fewer internal vetoes and reputational constraints.

### Strongest Counterarguments
1. **Constraint skepticism**: “rules-based order” may have been rhetoric; constraints were always weak when core interests were at stake.
2. **Causality reversal**: U.S. may be becoming more aggressive due to changing threat environment, not because constraints vanished.
3. **Overgeneralization from Iran**: a single conflict can mislead about broader systemic structure.
