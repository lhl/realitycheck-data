## Efforts to change the rules through executive action and federal legislation

A central “rules” document is President Trump’s March 25, 2025 executive order titled **“Preserving and Protecting the Integrity of American Elections.”** The order directed, among other things, the entity["organization","U.S. Election Assistance Commission","us election agency"] to require “documentary proof of United States citizenship” on the federal registration form and specified example documents (passport, REAL ID showing citizenship, military ID, or qualifying government ID with proof). citeturn12view0 It also directed federal departments to facilitate voter-citizenship verification, directed federal voter registration agencies to “assess citizenship” before providing registration forms in public assistance contexts, and contained provisions aimed at enforcing an Election Day ballot receipt deadline. citeturn12view0 The executive order further contemplated federal involvement in voting system standards and certification, including review/re-certification and rescission of prior certifications under new standards. citeturn12view0

Courts blocked key parts of this executive order. Reuters reports a federal judge permanently blocked the proof-of-citizenship registration requirement and found the order unlawfully intruded on state authority. citeturn11search23 The ACLU similarly describes the executive order as an unconstitutional power grab and highlights the court’s permanent injunction against the “show-your-papers” requirement for federal registration. citeturn11search4 Reuters also reported earlier preliminary blocking of significant portions of the order. citeturn11search6

## Emergency-powers scenarios and foreign-interference narratives as justification

Another rapidly developing line of activity involves draft or proposed emergency executive action that would claim foreign interference as a basis for extraordinary federal control over voting. The Washington Post reported that pro-Trump activists circulated a draft executive order claiming China interfered in 2020 as a basis to declare a national emergency and unlock extensive presidential power over voting. citeturn2search8 ABC News similarly reports on a 17-page draft order (which sources said Trump had reviewed) that would mandate voter ID, hand-counted ballots, and ban mail ballots—and that the draft relied on alleged Chinese interference “of which there is no evidence,” while citing an intelligence community assessment finding no evidence any foreign actor altered vote tallies or changed the 2020 election results. citeturn16view1 Democracy Docket published another version dated April 12, 2025 and reported legal experts said an order giving the president control over elections would be blatantly unconstitutional; it also described errors in the draft’s legal citations. citeturn16view2

A key background legal/administrative element here is Executive Order 13848 (2018), which declared a national emergency regarding foreign interference in U.S. elections under IEEPA and established processes for intelligence assessments and sanctions. citeturn15view0 A Federal Register presidential notice from 2025 continued that national emergency while explicitly stating there was “no evidence of a foreign power altering the outcomes or vote tabulation” in any U.S. election—language that conflicts with claims used in some emergency-power advocacy drafts that assert foreign alteration of vote outcomes. citeturn14search3
