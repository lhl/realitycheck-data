## Core Thesis

The United States is undergoing an authoritarian consolidation that follows well-documented historical patterns. Within roughly one year of taking office, the Trump2 administration has achieved an estimated 52% completion of the Project 2025 agenda (per the Project 2025 Observer tracker as of March 2026). The consolidation operates across multiple simultaneous axes — state violence, judicial capture, electoral manipulation, media control, institutional dismantling, corporate coercion, and graft — that are mutually reinforcing. The speed and breadth of this transformation suggests not democratic backsliding (a gradual, reversible process) but active demolition of republican self-governance.

## II. Axes of Consolidation

The authoritarian project operates along several axes simultaneously. These are not cleanly separable categories — they overlap and reinforce each other. State violence serves voter suppression as much as the election laws do. Media control enables all other axes by shaping the information environment. The key insight is that no single axis is the story; the *simultaneity* is the story.

### A. State Violence and Coercion (ICE/CBP as Paramilitary Force)

ICE and CBP function as the administration's paramilitary enforcement arm — deployed primarily in Blue states, operating with minimal legal constraint, and serving multiple functions simultaneously: immigration enforcement as stated pretext, but also coercion, voter suppression, and removal of agency from targeted populations.

**Operational pattern:**
- Thousands of officers flood a city (Operation Metro Surge in Minneapolis/St. Paul)
- Federal agents shoot civilians (Good, Jan 8; Pretti, Jan 24)
- Administration retroactively labels victims/bystanders as "domestic terrorists"
- Constitutional rights declared inapplicable ("don't count" during a "riot")
- AG memo redefines protest observation as terrorism
- SC has effectively ruled that appearance/ethnicity constitutes probable cause
- Citizens have begun carrying passports as a defensive measure — with no guarantee of protection

**Legal vacuum:**
- ICE can detain people broadly, including children; errors can result in citizen detention (GOV-2026-036, cred 0.80)
- ICE asserts authority for warrantless home entry using administrative (not judicial) warrants (GOV-2026-033, cred 0.90)
- Agents may disregard paper documents in favor of biometric checks (GOV-2026-035, cred 0.75)
- Deportation to third countries (not country of origin) with limited procedural safeguards (GOV-2026-039, cred 0.85)
- If ICE makes a serious mistake (wrongful removal), it generally cannot or will not bring the person back (GOV-2026-040, cred 0.55)

**Multiple functions of state violence:**
1. *Direct coercion*: suppress dissent, punish resistance, demonstrate state power
2. *Voter suppression*: terrorized communities don't vote; people afraid of deportation don't show up at polling places
3. *Agency removal*: populations that must carry papers and fear arbitrary detention are not free citizens; they are subjects
4. *Pretext generation*: each incident of resistance (or even observation) becomes justification for escalation
5. *Normalization*: daily presence of armed federal agents in civilian areas shifts what is considered normal

This is not a side effect — the multi-functionality is the design. The "immigration enforcement" framing provides deniable cover for what is operationally a domestic pacification campaign concentrated in politically hostile jurisdictions.

### B. Judicial Capture and Legal Impunity

The Supreme Court stacking was arguably the point of no return — the single intervention that disabled the republic's immune system.

**McConnell's sequential moves:**
- Blocked Merrick Garland for 11 months (2016) — unprecedented
- Confirmed Neil Gorsuch to stolen seat (2017)
- Rammed through Amy Coney Barrett weeks before the 2020 election — reversing his own stated principle
- Result: 6-3 conservative supermajority answerable to no democratic feedback mechanism

**Consequential rulings:**
- *Citizens United v. FEC* (2010): opened money floodgates, enabling oligarchic capture of politics
- *Trump v. United States* (2024): sweeping presidential immunity for "official acts" — the president is effectively above criminal law when acting through official channels
- Effective rulings allowing appearance/ethnicity as probable cause for immigration enforcement
- SC declined to block National Guard deployment to Chicago area, then stopped intervening

**Impeachment as dead letter:**
- First impeachment (Ukraine): McConnell ensured acquittal, blocked witnesses
- Second impeachment (Jan 6): McConnell said Trump was "practically and morally responsible" — then voted to acquit on procedural grounds
- McConnell explicitly said the criminal justice system should hold Trump accountable — then his Court made sure it couldn't

**The tragic irony**: McConnell, the person most responsible for disabling every institutional check on executive power, is now the last institutionalist holding the line against the SAVE America Act. He removed the republic's immune system in pursuit of short-term partisan advantage and is now horrified that the body can't fight off the infection. His own party is attacking his mental fitness for resisting.

### C. Electoral Manipulation

With state violence suppressing participation in targeted communities and judicial capture ensuring no legal remedy, the administration is also directly attacking the electoral mechanism itself.

**Legislative prong — The SAVE America Act:**
- Proof of citizenship to register, government photo ID to vote, photo ID copies for mail-in ballots
- Federal mandate overriding state election administration (from a party that claims states' rights)
- Estimated 20+ million disenfranchised, disproportionately poor and non-white
- 50 Republican Senate votes; blocked only by the 60-vote filibuster threshold
- McConnell refusing to advance from Rules Committee; Murkowski also opposed as "one-size-fits-all mandates from Washington"

**Procedural prong — Mail-in ballot elimination:**
- Trump at Georgia rally (2026-02-19): "We'll never lose a race for 50 years" if mail-in voting is eliminated
- The quiet part loud: this is explicitly about permanent Republican victories, not "election integrity"

**Executive prong:**
- Executive order targeting election activists (WaPo, 2026-02-26)
- Pattern of using executive power where legislation stalls

**The filibuster as last wall**: If McConnell is removed from his committee position, dies, or if the filibuster is broken for this bill, the electoral correction mechanism closes. Trump himself has pressured for filibuster elimination; Senate Majority Leader Thune says they're "not even close" to 60 votes for that — but the pressure is sustained.

### D. Media Control and Manufacturing Consent

A distinct axis from state violence, though they reinforce each other. The administration has pursued a strategy of ensuring major media platforms and outlets are controlled by executives aligned with or beholden to the ruling party.

**Known mechanisms:**
- CBS deal: structured to bring the network under aligned executive control
- TikTok deal: acquisition/restructuring that places the platform under favorable ownership
- Warner Bros deal: similar alignment of major media conglomerate

**Effect**: When state violence occurs (Minneapolis), the narrative is controlled. When corporate coercion occurs (Anthropic), coverage is shaped. When election laws are pushed, the framing favors the administration. This is Britt's characteristic #6 — not crude censorship but structural control of the information environment through ownership and executive alignment.

**What hasn't happened yet**: Flat-out arrest of journalists or formal censorship orders. The current approach is softer — ownership capture, advertiser pressure, "domestic terrorism" labels that chill coverage, and social media platform manipulation. This is more sophisticated than crude press suppression and arguably more effective.

### E. Corporate Coercion and Selective Enforcement

The government uses every available legal mechanism as a weapon of compliance, applied selectively: allied corporations are protected and enriched, while non-compliant ones face existential threats.

**The Anthropic model:**
- Company asserts contractual independence → threatened with supply chain risk designation, DPA invocation, total commercial cutoff
- Signal to all companies: unconditional compliance or destruction
- Treatment worse than actual adversary-linked firms (DeepSeek not designated)

**Broader pattern:**
- "Any lawful use" as the demanded standard — the government defines what's lawful and compliance is unconditional
- Other AI companies (OpenAI, xAI) immediately aligned with government terms, demonstrating the chilling effect
- The DPA (Defense Production Act) — a Korean War-era statute — invoked as coercive leverage against a domestic tech company

**The graft dimension** is covered in detail in Section II.H below — but the key point here is that corruption and corporate coercion are two faces of the same mechanism. Compliance is rewarded with contracts, access, and favorable regulation; resistance is punished with investigations, designations, and threats. The result is a loyalty economy where every major corporation must choose between complicity and destruction.

**Why this matters beyond "corruption":**

Corruption at this scale is not a side effect of authoritarian consolidation — it is a *mechanism* of it. It creates:

1. **Webs of mutual complicity**: Everyone who participates in the graft — the crypto buyers, the foreign governments providing jets, the companies seeking favorable treatment — becomes complicit and therefore invested in the regime's continuation. Exposure of their participation would harm them, so they become defenders.

2. **A loyalty economy**: Access to government contracts, favorable regulation, and protection from enforcement is contingent on financial and political loyalty. This replaces rule-of-law governance with patronage governance.

4. **Resource extraction**: The graft transfers public resources and national wealth to a narrow circle, weakening the state's capacity to serve the public while strengthening the ruling faction's ability to maintain power.

This is the pattern of every kleptocratic regime: corruption isn't a bug, it's a feature that binds the ruling coalition together through shared benefit and shared exposure.

## IV. The International Dimension

The domestic crisis cannot be understood in isolation. The rules-based international order was always a projection of domestic American constitutionalism outward. As the domestic version is demolished, the international version cannot survive.

**Smith's key inversion**: Noah Smith ("The Shape of the Multipolar World," March 2026) argues that Pax Americana actually *constrained* American power through self-imposed rules, norms, and institutional commitments — requiring the US to "restrain its use of military force in many cases, eschew territorial conquest" and treat smaller nations as equals in international bodies. The collapse of this order hasn't weakened the US as multipolar advocates expected — it has *unleashed* an unrestrained hegemon. Those who wished for constraints on American power got the opposite: an America freed from its own rules. This is the "be careful what you wish for" of multipolarity.

This connects directly to the domestic story. The same institutional self-constraints that governed American conduct abroad (treaty obligations, international law, multilateral institutions) were cognates of the domestic self-constraints now being demolished (constitutional limits, legislative process, judicial independence). When the domestic immune system was disabled (Section III), the international one went with it. The administration that treats its own citizens' constitutional rights as contingent is not going to honor treaty obligations.

**The emerging landscape** (Smith + Carney + RC claims):
- Carney's Davos thesis (GOV-2026-022, cred 0.75): great powers weaponizing economic integration — the international version of domestic corporate coercion
- Smith identifies a fragile "New Axis" of China, Russia, and Iran — but notes its weakness (China and Russia declined to defend Iran militarily, revealing Iran's marginal status)
- GOV-2026-021 (cred 0.55): structural rupture, not gradual transition, in the rules-based order
- GOV-2026-078 (cred 0.55): the rupture is substantially a cultural shift toward overt coercion as normal great-power behavior
- GOV-2026-080 (cred 0.50): no guarantee of convergence to a coherent new order — expect many partial "ordering attempts"
- GOV-2026-027 (cred 0.70): the post-WWII rules-based order is not returning to its previous form
- GOV-2026-068 (cred 0.50): U.S. sovereignty violations weaken legitimacy as defender of rules-based order
- Middle powers (Carney's concern, GOV-2026-024, cred 0.40) face an impossible bind: bilateral negotiation with an unrestrained hegemon means subordination (GOV-2026-023, cred 0.65), but coalition-building is slow and uncertain

**The void**: Neither Smith nor Carney articulates a clear replacement structure. Smith describes what's ending and what fragments emerge but not how global governance functions without American-led institutions or alternatives. This aligns with GOV-2026-080 — the likely near-term is not a new order but *incoherence*, multiple partial ordering attempts that don't converge.

The behavioral pattern is identical whether the target is a foreign government (Venezuela), an ally's territory (Greenland), a domestic tech company (Anthropic), or domestic civilians (Minneapolis): unilateral action, disproportionate force, treatment of any disagreement as an existential threat. This is what an *unconstrained* hegemon looks like — and it is uglier than the constrained version's critics imagined.

## VI. Assessment

### What Ball Gets Right (in "Clawed")
- The "death is a process" framing captures the temporal reality
- The contractual norms argument is solid — defense contracting has always involved private constraints
- The coercive overreach analysis is sharp and well-sourced
- The distinction between democratic and governmental control is genuinely important
- The second-order economic effects (cost of capital, AI competitiveness) are plausible

### What Ball's Framing Misses
- **Scope**: He treats the Anthropic dispute as the primary lens; it is one axis of a much broader consolidation
- **The bodily dimension**: Property rights and contracting norms while people are being shot on the streets of Minneapolis
- **Active demolition vs. natural death**: His hospice metaphor is too gentle. This is not a body dying of natural causes — it's being smothered. The mechanisms aren't just decaying; they're being systematically dismantled by people who view them as obstacles
- **Electoral dimension**: He doesn't address the simultaneous attack on voting rights
- **Media control**: He doesn't address the structural capture of media
- **Project 2025 as systematic agenda**: This isn't ad hoc or improvised — it's executing a documented plan at 52% completion
- **McConnell's culpability**: He doesn't connect the judicial capture to the current moment

### The Speed Question
The most alarming fact may be the pace: ~52% of Project 2025 in under one year. At this rate, the question is not whether the consolidation succeeds but whether any of the remaining counter-mechanisms (filibuster, McConnell's holdout, mid-level judiciary, state-level resistance, corporate resistance, public protest) can slow it enough for the 2026 midterms to matter — assuming those midterms are conducted fairly, which is precisely what is being attacked.

### Open Questions
1. **Filibuster timeline**: How long before it's broken for the SAVE America Act? McConnell is 84 and under siege.
2. **Iran as emergency pretext**: Does war trigger domestic emergency powers?
3. **Corporate compliance cascade**: Has the Anthropic example successfully chilled all corporate independence?
4. **State-level resistance**: Can blue states meaningfully resist federal overreach, or does judicial capture foreclose that?
5. **Dissident targeting**: State violence is concentrated on immigrants and their communities; protest observers and bystanders are being labeled terrorists; but flat-out arrest of political dissidents hasn't happened yet. When does this cross?
6. **AI takeoff as wildcard**: If AI capabilities continue to accelerate, does this change the power dynamics? The administration may have more leverage if U.S. AI is too good to forgo — but other nations will develop independent capabilities precisely because of U.S. unreliability.
7. **Post-republic scenarios**: Ball says whatever comes next will be "deeply intertwined with AI." Is there a path to a new founding, or are we transitioning into something darker?
